Public Sector Accounting · Chapter 7

Financial officers & what each one does

Eight officers control Nigeria's public money, from the political appointee at the top to the officer watching a single ministry's vote-heads day to day. This guide sets out what each one actually does — condensed from the study text's own (very long) function lists — and how they relate to each other.

§7.1 · Introduction

0 · The two kinds of officer

Officials managing government funds and property fall into two categories:

Career officials

Attained their positions by experience, hard work and efficiency, and cannot be arbitrarily removed — the AGF, the AuGF, accounting officers, sub-accounting officers, Federal Pay Officers, revenue collectors and the Local Government Secretary.

Political officials

Occupy their positions by political appointment, not necessarily with field experience — the Federal Minister of Finance, a State Commissioner for Finance, a Local Government Chairman and Councillors.

All government expenditure must be properly authorised — the authority that empowers an officer controlling a vote to spend is a warrant, always issued and signed by the Minister of Finance. See the full warrant system.

§7.2 · Political appointment

1 · The Minister of Finance

Appointed by the President (after Senate approval) — responsible for the control and management of the Federation's public funds.

  • Prepares the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure.
  • Formulates fiscal and monetary policy.
  • Mobilises foreign and domestic financial resources for development.
  • Maintains a favourable balance of payments and adequate foreign exchange reserves.
  • Stabilises the value of the naira, internally and externally.
  • Supervises the allocation of revenue to the three tiers of government.
  • Liaises with international bodies (ECOWAS, the UN, the EU, the AU) to accelerate the development of Nigerian financial institutions.
§7.3 · Financial Regulations 106 & 107 (2009)

2 · The Accountant-General of the Federation (AGF)

The Chief Accounting Officer for the Federation's receipts and payments — general supervisor of every MDA's accounts, and preparer of the nation's annual financial statements.

Powers (FR 106)

  • Access to the books/records of every ministry at any reasonable time.
  • Power to demand information/explanation needed for his duties.
  • Power to carry out special/ad-hoc investigations in any MDA.

Functions (FR 107) — the essentials

  • Supervises MDA accounts; collates, prepares and publishes the government's statutory financial statements.
  • Maintains and operates the CRF, Development Fund, Contingency Fund and the Federation Account itself.
  • Manages federal government investments and services public debt/loans.
  • Runs Federal Pay Offices in every state capital; inspects MDA books for compliance.
  • Approves accounting codes, internal-audit guides and stock-verification manuals.
  • Investigates fraud, loss of funds/stores and other financial malpractice.
  • Issues Treasury Circulars and Treasury-numbered forms; formulates federal accounting policy.
  • Supervises computerisation of MDA accounting systems; trains accounts/internal-audit staff.
§7.4 · 1999 Constitution, ss.84–87 · FR 109

3 · The Auditor-General for the Federation (AuGF)

Audits and reports on the public accounts of the Federation. At the end of audit, states whether: accounts were properly kept; all public funds were fully accounted for; money was expended as authorised; and essential records adequately safeguard public property.

Appointment & tenure

  • Appointed by the President on the Federal Civil Service Commission's recommendation, confirmed by the Senate.
  • Leaves office on 35 years' service or age 60, whichever is earlier — or for ill-health, death or gross misconduct.
  • Removal (s.87(1)): by the President, acting on an address supported by a two-thirds majority of the Senate.

The four types of audit (FR 109)

TypeChecks
Financial auditAccounts satisfactorily and faithfully kept
Appropriation auditFunds expended as appropriated by the National Assembly
Financial control auditProcedures observed in tendering, contracts and storekeeping
Value-for-money (performance) auditEconomy, efficiency and effectiveness of projects/programmes
The classic exam trap

The AuGF does not directly audit statutory corporations, commissions or agencies (s.85(3)). He supplies them a list of approved external auditors, comments on their audited accounts, and conducts periodic checks (s.85(4)) — the statutory audit itself is done by the firm the corporation appoints.

Independence

  • s.85(6) — not subject to the direction or control of any other authority or person.
  • s.87(2) — cannot be removed before retiring age except for incapacity or misconduct.
  • s.84(3)–(4) — remuneration is a direct CRF charge, and cannot be altered to his detriment after appointment.

Reports to the National Assembly within 90 days of receiving the AGF's financial statements (s.85(5)). See every other numeric threshold →

§7.5 · 1999 Constitution, s.126(1)

4 · The Auditor-General for a State

Appointed by the State Governor on the State Civil Service Commission's recommendation, confirmed by the State House of Assembly.

  • Ensures public establishments keep adequate accounts and that public money is fully accounted for.
  • Audits the public accounts of the State, and of all State officers and courts.
  • Certifies computations of pensions/gratuities of retiring officers.
  • Monitors and evaluates government projects.
  • Liaises with the State House of Assembly's Public Accounts Committee.
  • Recommends external-auditor remuneration for State corporations/commissions, and reviews their reports.
  • Special audits across the executive, legislature and judiciary of the State.
§7.6 · Permanent Secretaries of the MDAs

5 · Accounting officers

The Permanent Secretary of a ministry (or head of an extra-ministerial office), in full control of the human, material and financial resources of the organisation, and responsible for its day-to-day financial affairs.

General duties

  • Safeguards public funds and the regularity/propriety of expenditure under his control — accountability continues even after leaving office.
  • Establishes and maintains proper budgetary/accounting systems for internal control and transparency.
  • Renders monthly returns/transcripts to the AGF.
  • Appears personally before the Public Accounts Committee to answer audit queries.
  • Ensures accurate collection/accounting of public money, and that revenue collected is remitted promptly, never spent directly.
  • Reports and investigates any loss of revenue promptly.

Special duties under the Public Procurement Act

  • Presides over the entity's Tenders' Board, Procurement Planning Committee and Procurement Committee; constitutes the Procurement Evaluation Committee.
  • Ensures adequate appropriation for procurement is in the annual budget.
  • Renders annual procurement returns to the Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP).
  • Personally liable for any breach of the Act by the organisation — whether or not he caused it directly, even if a subordinate or delegate is actually responsible.
Not called "surcharge" here — but it is one

The chapter never uses the word "surcharge" for this personal liability, but the mechanism is identical: an officer answers personally for a loss or breach on his watch, regardless of who actually caused it.

§7.7 · Examples: Sub-Treasurer, FPO, PPO, CAPO, DFA

6 · Sub-accounting officers

Entrusted with the receipt, custody and disbursement of public funds — required to maintain a recognised cash book plus whatever other books the AGF prescribes. Examples: the Sub-Treasurer of the Federation, Federal Pay Officer (FPO), Police Pay Officer (PPO), Customs Area Pay Officer (CAPO), Director of Finance and Accounts (DFA).

  • Establishes the AGF's prescribed system of accounts; supervises prompt collection of public revenue.
  • Promptly posts all receipts to the correct heads/sub-heads.
  • Safeguards public funds, securities, stamps and valuable documents.
  • Supervises staff entrusted with public funds; guards against fraud, embezzlement and carelessness.
  • Ensures no payment is made without proper authorisation; promptly charges all disbursements.
  • Reconciles cash/stamps in his care against the cash book and stamp register.
  • Makes good any minor (non-fraud) deficiency personally, reporting it in writing to the Minister of Finance.
  • Maintains the cash book.
§7.8 · Within a single ministry's vote

7 · Officer controlling expenditure

In charge of the vote-heads of a single ministry or extra-ministerial department — monitors government expenditure and prevents extra-budgetary spending.

  • Ensures no payment is made without proper authority.
  • Promptly charges all disbursements to the correct heads/sub-heads; keeps books up to date.
  • Produces cash, stamps etc. on demand from the AGF or AuGF.
  • Confirms funds are available under the right head/sub-head before specific vouchers are paid.
  • Secures the custody of public funds.
  • Maintains the vote book.
§7.9 · Budget Office of the Federation

8 · The Budget Office

Through which the Minister of Finance monitors and evaluates budget implementation, assesses fiscal-target attainment, and reports to the Fiscal Responsibility Commission and the National Assembly's Joint Finance Committee.

  • Develops economic assumptions/forecasts; issues budget guidelines to MDAs.
  • Liaises between the Presidency and MDAs during budget preparation.
  • Compiles revenue/expenditure estimates; drafts the budget speech.
  • Supervises budget implementation; monitors/evaluates funded programmes.
  • Assesses the budget's economic impact; researches budget utilisation against national objectives.
Putting it together · Who answers to whom

9 · Who answers to whom

Officer controlling expenditure
Charges expenditure and keeps the vote book current, within a single ministry — produces cash/records to the AGF or AuGF on demand.
Sub-accounting officer
Runs a specific cash office (a Pay Office, say), maintaining the cash book — answers to the accounting officer and the AGF.
Accounting officer
The Permanent Secretary — in overall control of one MDA, answers personally to the Public Accounts Committee, renders returns to the AGF.
Accountant-General
Consolidates and supervises across every MDA, operates the CRF/DF/CF/Federation Account, prepares the nation's own financial statements.
Auditor-General
Sits outside the accounting hierarchy entirely — audits what the AGF and every accounting officer produced, reports directly to the National Assembly, answerable to no other authority.
Minister of Finance
The political head — sets fiscal policy, signs every warrant that releases money in the first place, and receives the Budget Office's reports.
