1 · Pension & gratuity

The single most exam-friendly numeric fact in the whole syllabus — and the one most often deliberately swapped between options.

The core rule

5 years' service is the minimum to qualify for gratuity alone ("withdrawal of service"). 10 years' service is the minimum to qualify for both gratuity and pension ("retirement"). Pension itself is then payable for a minimum of 5 years or till death, whichever is longer.

Armed Forces Pension Act

  • Government contributes 12.5%, military staff 8% of basic salary + allowances.
  • War-service years count double; a period of ordinary service longer than 6 months rounds up to a full year.

Pension Reform Act 2014 (Contributory Pension Scheme)

  • Minimum contribution: employer 10% + employee 8% = 18% of monthly emolument (basic + housing + transport only).
  • If the employer takes on full responsibility alone, it must contribute at least 20%.
  • Scheme applies to private-sector employers with 15 or more employees. Under 3 employees, or self-employed → Micro Pension Plan instead.
  • Employer must maintain group life insurance of at least 3× annual total emolument.
  • Exemption available to a pre-25 June 2004 scheme member with 3 years or fewer left to retirement.
  • RSA (Retirement Savings Account) normal withdrawal age: 50 years (earlier only on medical incapacity/permanent disability, or under the terms of employment).
  • An early retiree unable to secure work within 4 months may withdraw a lump sum, capped at 25% of the RSA balance, only after 6 months of retirement.
  • Voluntary contributions are taxed if withdrawn within 5 years.
  • Employer must remit contributions within 7 working days of paying salary; late remittance penalty is at least 2% of the unpaid amount per month.
  • A new employee must open an RSA within 6 months of starting duty, else the employer/PFA opens a nominal one.
  • An RSA may be transferred between PFAs at most once a year.
  • Fitness-for-work review by the Medical Board: every 2 years.
  • A Pension Fund Custodian (PFC) must notify the PFA within 24 hours of receiving a contribution, and must report an employer's default beyond 14 days to the Commission.

PenCom & the Pension Fund Custodian

  • PenCom's part-time Chairman and Director-General each require at least 20 years' relevant experience (plus a first degree for the Chairman); the 4 full-time Commissioners each require the same 20-year minimum.
  • A PFC's minimum net worth is ₦25,000,000,000.
  • Statutory Reserve Fund = 12.5% of the PFA's net profit after tax.
  • Pension Protection Fund is funded partly by 1% of the Federal public service's total monthly wage bill.
  • A PFA/PFC licence revocation requires at least 7 calendar days' notice.

Offences & penalties

  • General offence: fine of at least ₦250,000 or imprisonment of at least 1 year.
  • A PFA/PFC reimbursing a staff member's fine: at least ₦5,000,000.
  • Misappropriation of pension funds: 10 years' imprisonment plus a fine of 3× the amount involved.
  • PFC-specific offence: ₦10,000,000 minimum (or the lesser combination of ₦5,000,000 and 5 years).
  • False or misleading information: at least ₦200,000 or 3 years, plus a continuing penalty of ₦100,000 a day.

Micro Pension Plan

  • Eligible from age 18.
  • Contributions split 40% contingent / 60% retirement.
  • Contingent portion accessible 3 months after the first contribution, thereafter at most once a week, paid within 2 working days.
  • Admin fee: ₦80 on contributions of ₦4,000 or more, ₦20 below that.
  • EFCC must be notified of single lodgements of ₦5,000,000 or more.

The Constitution's own pension rules

Section 173(3): pensions are reviewed every 5 years, or with any civil-service salary review, whichever comes first. Section 173(4): pensions are not taxed.

Ch 5 · Employee Benefits (IPSAS 39)

2 · Employee benefits

  • The 12-month test is what separates a short-term benefit (undiscounted) from an "other long-term" benefit (discounted).
  • Under the old IPSAS 25, a transitional liability could be spread over up to 5 years.
  • Retirement-benefit-plan disclosure flags any single investment exceeding 5% of net assets available for benefits (or 5% of any one class of security).
Ch 2 & Ch 6 · Fiscal framework & budgeting

3 · Budgeting & the MTEF

  • Fiscal Responsibility Act 2007 deficit ceiling: ≤3% of GDP (an excess needs National Assembly approval).
  • Scheduled corporations must remit 80% of their operating surplus to the Consolidated Revenue Fund.
  • The first Medium-Term Expenditure Framework had to be laid before the National Assembly within 6 months of the Act's commencement; every MTEF after that must be laid at least 4 months before the new financial year.
  • The Minister must present the MTEF to the Federal Executive Council before the end of the second quarter of each year.
  • The annual budget must be accompanied by an 18-month actual-vs-budget performance report, running up to June of the preceding year.
  • A Provisional General Warrant — issued before the Appropriation Act is passed — is capped at the previous year's level and is valid for a maximum of 6 months (Constitution s.82). The Development Fund's own provisional warrant carries the same 6-month cap.

Ch 7 · Financial officers & warrants

4 · Financial officers & warrants

  • The Auditor-General for the Federation (s.86(1)) leaves office at 35 years' service or age 60, whichever comes first.
  • Removing the AuGF (s.87(1)) needs the President to act on an address supported by a two-thirds majority of the Senate.
  • The AuGF must submit the report to the National Assembly within 90 days of receiving the Accountant-General's financial statements (s.85(5)).
  • A State Auditor-General (s.126(1)) is appointed by the Governor on the State Civil Service Commission's recommendation, confirmed by the State House of Assembly.
  • Every warrant is issued in 2 copies — original to the Accountant-General of the Federation, duplicate to the Auditor-General — and gazetted.
Ch 10 · Public Procurement Act

5 · Public procurement — the richest single topic for numbers

  • Founding legislation: the Public Procurement Act, 2007.
  • The National Council on Public Procurement is chaired by the Minister of Finance, plus 6 part-time members.
  • A "Request for Quotations" needs at least 3 written quotations from unrelated suppliers.
  • A Permanent Secretary/parastatal CEO may approve a contract up to ₦5,000,000 without open tender, provided at least 3 written quotations are obtained and a quarterly return is made.
  • Procurement records are retained for 7 years from project completion/handover.
  • Retention fee on a contract: around 5% of the contract sum, withheld for 6 months after completion.
  • Electronic contractor payment has been mandatory since January 2009.
  • A complaint to the accounting officer must be answered within 15 working days; an escalation to the Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP) is decided within 21 working days.
  • Penalty for an offence under the Act: a natural person gets at least 5 years' imprisonment with no option of a fine; a body corporate gets a fine of at least 25% of the procurement value, plus possible debarment.

Ch 11 · Stores, losses & the Board of Survey

6 · Stores, losses & the Board of Survey

  • A Board of Survey has 3 officers: a President at Grade Level 08 or above, and at least 2 members at Grade Level 06 or above.
  • An accounting officer may personally surcharge a cash loss of up to ₦50,000 where no fraud is involved.
  • A loss is reported on Treasury Form 146, prepared in quintuplicate (5 copies).

Ch 12 · Local government finance

8 · Local government finance

  • 774 local government councils share the 20.60% Federation Account tranche.
  • Councils commonly receive 10% of a State's internally generated revenue (s.162(7)).
  • Spending is capped by an IGR band — at the top band (IGR above ₦2m), a Chairman may approve up to ₦250,000, a Vice-Chairman up to ₦50,000, a Head of Personnel Management up to ₦10,000, and a Head of Department up to ₦5,000 (two further, lower IGR bands set smaller limits again). Every approval must be reported within a week.
Ch 13 · Cash, imprest & records

9 · Cash, imprest & records

  • An imprest holder must be at least Grade Level 04.
  • Cash of ₦50,000 or more must be banked, and petty-cash approval by the accounting officer is capped at the same ₦50,000.
  • Collectors' cash books and vouchers are retained for 7 years; Local Purchase Order and transport-warrant copies for 2 years.
Ch 22 · Ethics · ICPC · EFCC · CCB

10 · Ethics, ICPC, EFCC & the Code of Conduct Bureau

  • Most ICPC gratification/bribery offences carry 7 years' imprisonment; bribery of a public officer specifically is 5 years with hard labour; personal-gratification misuse of office is 5 years, no fine option; postal offences run 3–7 years.
  • Code of Conduct Bureau: the Chairman serves 5 years (renewable once); other members serve 4 years (renewable once).
  • Asset declaration is due within 3 months of taking office, then every 4 years, and again at the end of the term.
  • EFCC Commissioners are appointed by the National Assembly for 3 years, renewable.
  • A cash transaction of ₦5,000,000 or more (individual — higher for a corporate body) is reportable under money-laundering rules within 7 days; records are preserved for 10 years.
  • General economic/financial crime carries 15–25 years' imprisonment.
  • The FRCN receives annual reports within 60 days of Board approval (cross-referenced in Chapter 1).

11 · Audit-query response times

Arguably the single richest "numbers" table in the whole syllabus: every type of audit query carries its own statutory number of days to respond. Learn the table as a table, not as isolated facts — several pairs are only a few days apart and are exactly what an exam option swaps.

The 5/7/14/21/30/42-day ladder

Notice the pattern: the shortest windows (5–7 days) attach to cash and revenue-reply failures — the most immediately reversible/traceable issues. 21 days is the default for most substantive irregularities. 30 and 42 days are reserved for the queries that genuinely need more time to investigate (non-performance of work, poor quality). If an option looks plausible but the number feels "too short" for a complex query like poor-quality work, it is probably the wrong pairing.

Cross-cutting · Easily-swapped pairs

12 · Numbers examiners love to swap

5 years vs 10 years

5 years of service → gratuity only. 10 years of service → gratuity and pension. An option will happily offer "10 years for gratuity" as bait.

2014 vs 2016

Nigeria's Cash Basis IPSAS took effect 2014; the Accrual Basis suite took effect 2016. See the IPSAS guide for the full timeline.

10% employer + 8% employee (2014 Act) vs the superseded 7.5% + 7.5% (2004 Act)

The current Pension Reform Act 2014 minimums are asymmetric (10%/8%); the 2004 Act it replaced was symmetric (7.5%/7.5%). A past-question option lifted from an older diet may still use the 2004 figures.

6-month provisional warrant vs 90-day audit report

The Provisional General Warrant cap (6 months, Constitution s.82) and the Auditor-General's own reporting deadline (90 days, s.85(5)) are two completely different clocks that a hurried read can conflate.

CCB Chairman (5 years) vs CCB members (4 years)

Both renewable once — but only once each, and the Chairman's term is the longer of the two.
