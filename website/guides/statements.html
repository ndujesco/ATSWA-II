Cash basis vs accrual basis, statement by statement

Every statement the Nigerian public sector prepares, cash basis (Chapter 14) and accrual basis (Chapter 16) side by side — what each one reports, what changes between the two bases, and every past exam question on the topic.

Cash basis since: 1 Jan 2014Accrual basis since: 1 Jan 2016Governing standards: Cash Basis IPSAS · IPSAS 1
Core · The one idea everything else follows from

What actually changes between the two bases

Both bases eventually report the same cash. The entire difference is when a revenue or expense is recognised, and therefore what else besides cash ends up on the statements.

Cash basis
  • Revenue recognised when received; expenditure when paid
  • No receivables, no payables, no PP&E, no depreciation, no long-term liabilities
  • Reports only financial assets/liabilities from cash transactions — cash, investments, advances, deposits
  • Can be manipulated by timing a payment past year-end
Accrual basis
  • Revenue recognised when earned; expenses when incurred — whether or not cash has moved
  • Full balance sheet: PP&E, receivables, payables, borrowings, pension obligations, all visible
  • Shows the true cost of a service (via depreciation) and the true level of public debt
  • Timing a payment no longer changes the reported result
Cash is cash, whatever the basis

The closing cash balance is identical either way — what accrual accounting changes is everything around the cash (assets owned, amounts owed, revenue earned but not yet collected), never the cash figure itself. A Statement of Cash Flows prepared under accrual IPSAS reconciles to the exact same closing cash a Statement of Cash Receipts and Payments would show for the same entity.

Ch 14 · Required under the Cash Basis IPSAS

The cash-basis statements

1. Statement of Cash Receipts and Payments

principal statement

All cash received and paid during the period, classified by nature, opening and closing cash and cash equivalents. Revenue is recognised when received, expenditure when paid — whatever period the transaction actually relates to.

Worked-example checkpoint: total receipts less total payments gives the net cash flow for the year; add the opening cash balance and the total must agree with cash and cash equivalents in the Statement of Assets and Liabilities. Always perform this check before submitting an answer.

2. Statement of Assets and Liabilities

Limited by design: only cash, investments, advances and deposits — no PP&E, no receivables, no payables, because none of those are recognised under the cash basis. Net assets are represented by the fund balances (the CRF balance plus the Capital Development Fund balance, which is usually the balancing figure).

The single most common error in this question

Including buildings, roads, vehicles or trade payables in a cash-basis Statement of Assets and Liabilities. Those only ever appear under accrual IPSAS, in the Statement of Financial Position.

3. Statement of Consolidated Revenue Fund

Opening balance, revenue, recurrent expenditure and transfers (to the Development Fund, to the Contingency Fund), giving the closing CRF balance. See the full CRF guide for everything that feeds and finances this fund.

4. Statement of Capital Development Fund

Opening balance, transfers from the CRF, capital receipts, capital expenditure, and the closing balance — the capital-side mirror of the CRF statement above.

5. Statement of Comparison of Budget and Actual Amounts

required under both bases

Original budget, final budget (after any supplementary appropriation or virement), actual amounts on a comparable basis, and the variance — with material differences explained. This is the public sector's own signature statement: because the budget is a legal authority, not a management plan, reporting against it is an obligation.

Reading a variance: favourable on receipts when actual exceeds budget; favourable on payments when actual is below budget. Examiners award most marks for commentary that connects two variances (e.g. a revenue shortfall absorbed entirely by the capital budget), not for reciting each line separately.

6. Notes to the financial statements

Accounting policies (including the basis itself); composition of cash and cash equivalents; restrictions on cash/borrowing facilities; the basis of the budget and explanation of variances; third-party payments made on the entity's behalf; undrawn borrowing facilities; supporting analyses of every principal line; consolidated entities; related-party disclosures.

Limitations of the cash basis

  • Ignores receivables/payables — unpaid contractors and uncollected taxes are invisible
  • Omits non-current assets and depreciation — the true cost of using assets never enters the accounts
  • Open to manipulation by timing a payment past year-end
  • Does not disclose the full cost of a service, so economy/efficiency can't be assessed
  • Shows only cash liabilities — understates real public debt
  • Not comparable with accrual-based reporting elsewhere
Ch 16 · Required under IPSAS 1

The accrual-basis statements

1. Statement of Financial Performance

the "income statement"

Revenue earned and expenses incurred during the period, whether or not cash has moved, giving the surplus or deficit for the year. Minimum line items include revenue from operating activities, finance cost, and surplus/deficit from operating activities — but not "changes in cash and cash equivalents", which belongs on the Statement of Cash Flows instead.

2. Statement of Financial Position

the "balance sheet"

Assets, liabilities and net assets/equity at the reporting date — including property, plant and equipment, receivables and payables, none of which exist under the cash basis. IPSAS replaces the term "Balance Sheet" with this name.

3. Statement of Changes in Net Assets/Equity

The movement in each component of net assets — accumulated surplus, revaluation reserve, other reserves — between the opening and closing dates. Captures the surplus/deficit for the year together with items recognised directly in net assets.

Where a revaluation gain goes

A gain on revaluation of property, and the remeasurement of a defined benefit obligation (IPSAS 39), are both recognised directly in this statement — never routed through the Statement of Financial Performance. Doing so misstates both statements at once.

4. Statement of Cash Flows

Cash flows classified into operating, investing and financing activities (IPSAS 2). Operating flows may use the direct method (gross receipts/payments) or the indirect method (surplus adjusted for non-cash items and working-capital movements) — IPSAS encourages direct, but examinations usually set the indirect method, since it tests the cash/accrual distinction directly: add back depreciation and finance cost, deduct investment income, then adjust for the movement in every working-capital line.

5. Statement of Comparison of Budget and Actual Amounts

IPSAS 24

Identical requirement to the cash-basis version above — original budget, final budget, actual on a comparable basis, variance explained — just prepared on accrual figures.

6. Notes

Significant accounting policies plus supporting analysis of every principal figure — the same role as under the cash basis, just covering a much larger set of recognised items.

Advantages of accrual reporting

  • Discloses the full financial position — what government owns and owes
  • Shows the true cost of a service via depreciation — enables cost-per-unit and value-for-money analysis
  • Forces proper asset registers, making deterioration visible
  • Discloses the real level of public debt, including pension liabilities
  • Removes the year-end payment-timing manipulation the cash basis allows
  • Improves comparability with other governments and the private sector
  • Supports buy-vs-lease, maintain-vs-replace and contract-out decisions cash information can't

Against these: the cost and complexity of accrual accounting, the judgement (and scope for manipulation of that judgement) it requires, and the genuine difficulty of valuing heritage/infrastructure assets with no market. A balanced answer acknowledges both sides before concluding.

A change between bases (cash to accrual, say) is itself a change in accounting policy — the very reason Chapter 15's transitional relief exists.

A third way · The modified bases

Modified cash and modified accrual basis

Two hybrid bases sit between the pure cash and pure accrual positions — and the past papers are not entirely consistent about which name goes with which combination, so learn the combination, not just the label.

BasisRevenue recognisedExpenditure recognised
Modified cash basisCash (when received)Accrual (when incurred)
Modified accrual basisAccrual (when earned)Cash (when paid)
Commitment basis—Recorded once evidenced by a purchase order or contract, before cash even moves
A genuine inconsistency in the past papers

Two diets (2016-09, 2024-03) give "Modified Cash Basis" as the answer for "revenue on cash, expenditure on accrual" — but a separate diet's own MCQ (2020-03 Q27) explicitly defines "Modified Accrual Basis" as exactly that same combination, and 2020-09's short-answer key agrees with 2020-03. Six facts worth over-learning

  1. Both bases report the same closing cash — accrual accounting adds everything around the cash, it never changes the cash itself.
  2. "Statement of Financial Performance" and "Statement of Financial Position" are IPSAS's own names for what private-sector accounting calls the income statement and the balance sheet.
  3. The Statement of Comparison of Budget and Actual Amounts is required under both bases — it is the one statement cash and accrual reporting have in common, and a frequently-omitted item on "list the required statements" questions.
  4. A revaluation gain, and a defined-benefit remeasurement, are recognised in the Statement of Changes in Net Assets/Equity — never in the Statement of Financial Performance.
  5. Examiners set the indirect method for the Statement of Cash Flows precisely because reconstructing cash from an accrual surplus tests whether you understand the basis difference at all.
  6. Learn combinations, not labels, for the modified bases — the past papers themselves don't agree on which name goes with "revenue cash, expenditure accrual".
