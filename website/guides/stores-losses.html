Public Sector Accounting · Chapter 11

Stores, losses & the Board of Survey

The single most-repeated topic across every PS past paper on this site — more short-answer and objective questions trace back to this one chapter than to any other. This guide pulls together the three things examiners keep asking: which document does what, how a Board of Survey differs from a Board of Enquiry, and exactly what happens, step by step, when cash or stores go missing.

§11.1 · Classification

1 · Classifying stores — two independent splits

Every store falls into one class on when it's charged to the vote, and a separate class on whether it's returnable. These are two different axes, not four alternatives — a single item is one of each.

ClassMeaningCharged to the vote
AllocatedBought for, and charged directly to, a particular vote or jobImmediately, on purchase — no store ledger value carried
UnallocatedBought in bulk, held for future issue to whichever vote needs it — stationery, fuel, spare parts, drugsOn issue — held at cost in the store ledger until then
ExpendableConsumed in use, not returnable — stationery, cleaning materials, drugs, fuelWritten off on issue
Non-expendableDurable, returnable after use — tools, furniture, equipment, vehiclesCarried on an inventory/asset register until disposal
The recurring one-line test

"Store items purchased for general use, whose eventual vote isn't known at purchase" → unallocated. If a past question just says "for accounting purposes stores are classified into two" without qualifying which axis, it means allocated / unallocated — that pairing, not expendable/non-expendable, is the one the study text presents as the primary accounting split.

§11.2, §11.6 · Records and documents

2 · Document glossary — "which document does X?"

This exact phrasing — name the document that does a specific job — is the most common single question type on this chapter. Learn the table, not just the individual entries; several are deliberately similar-sounding.

DocumentWhat it does

Store Receipt Voucher (SRV)Evidences receipt into store after inspection — authority for the receipt side of the ledger
Store Issue Voucher (SIV)Authorises and evidences issue from store — authority for the issue side

The pairing to never mix up

Bin card = storekeeper, quantity only. Store ledger = accounts section, quantity and value. They are two independent records of the same movement, kept by different officers, and made to agree — the entire point is that neither officer can quietly alter the record alone.

§11.3 · Stock verification

3 · Stock verification — surplus vs shortage

Carried out by stock verifiers independent of the storekeeper, periodically and without notice, and in full at year end — to confirm existence/quantity, catch pilferage, flag obsolete items for write-off, test the ledgers, and confirm safe custody.

Surplus

Physical count exceeds the record. Usually an unrecorded receipt or a double-recorded issue. Investigated, then the ledger is adjusted by a Store Adjustment Voucher.

Shortage

Record exceeds the physical count. Treated as a loss — the §11.4 procedure applies. Within a normal wastage allowance, it may be written off on the accounting officer's own certificate; beyond it, a Board of Enquiry is needed.

§11.5 · Boards of Survey and Boards of Enquiry

4 · Board of Survey vs Board of Enquiry

The one-line test

A Board of Survey verifies what is there. A Board of Enquiry investigates what is missing.

Board of Survey
  • When: routinely — year end, or when a cash office/store changes hands
  • Convened by: the Accountant-General of the Federation
  • Composition: a President (Grade Level 08+) and at least two members (Grade Level 06+) — a single-member board only where unavoidable, with the reason stated on the report
  • Does: counts cash/stamps against the cash book; confirms bank balances by direct bank certificate; verifies stores against ledgers/bin cards; examines unserviceable/obsolete/surplus items and recommends disposal; checks safe custody; reports discrepancies
  • Output: a survey report to the accounting officer, copied to the Accountant-General and Auditor-General
Board of Enquiry
  • When: only on the occurrence of a specific loss
  • Composition: senior officers independent of where the loss occurred, sometimes with external/professional members
  • Does: establishes the amount, the cause, whether Financial Regulations were followed, whether negligence contributed, and who is responsible
  • Output: findings and recommendations on surcharge, discipline and prevention, to the accounting officer

Annual Board of Survey (year end, supports the cash/stores figures in the financial statements) vs special Board of Survey (on a change of custody, so the incoming officer takes over a verified balance and neither officer can be blamed for the other's shortfall) are the two occasions the study text names for convening one.

§11.4 · Losses of cash and stores

5 · What actually happens when something goes missing

This sequence is tested both as a "state the procedure" essay and as individual short-answer/objective fragments of it. Learn it as one chain.

  1. The officer who discovers the loss reports it immediately, in writing, to the supervising officer and the accounting officer.
  2. The accounting officer reports at once to the Accountant-General and the Auditor-General — and to the Police if theft or fraud is suspected.
  3. The loss is entered in the Register of Losses with full particulars.
  4. The officer suspected of responsibility may be interdicted pending investigation.
  5. A Board of Enquiry is constituted to establish the amount, cause, compliance with Financial Regulations, negligence and responsibility.
  6. The Board reports its findings and recommendations to the accounting officer.
  7. Where negligence or misconduct is established, the officer is surcharged and faces discipline — and prosecution, if the conduct is criminal.
  8. The irrecoverable balance is written off by whichever authority is competent for the amount — the accounting officer, the Minister of Finance, or the National Assembly.
  9. The control weakness that allowed the loss is identified and remedied.
Write-off and surcharge are two different decisions

Write-off clears the accounting record. Surcharge fixes personal liability. One does not discharge the other — an officer can be surcharged even after the loss itself has been written off. A question on "the treatment of a loss" wants both halves of this, explicitly.

§11.9 · Loss of government fund

6 · The accounting officer's own power, and the Federal Losses Committee

The accounting officer's surcharge threshold

Where a cash loss of ₦50,000 or less involves no fraud, the accounting officer may personally surcharge the responsible officer up to the full amount, and is personally responsible for seeing it done. (See the full numeric picture, alongside every other threshold in the syllabus, in PS by the numbers.)

The officer in whose office the loss occurred must report immediately to the head of unit, report to the Police if fraud/theft is suspected, complete Treasury Form 146 Part II in quintuplicate, fix any control weakness found, and ensure the entries are made.

Federal Losses Committee

A standing committee considering every case of loss of cash, stores and vehicles. Chaired by a representative of the Auditor-General, with representatives of the Accountant-General, the administration department of the ministry/agency concerned, the Inspector-General of Police, and the EFCC; the Inspectorate Department of the Accountant-General's office is the secretariat. It examines the Board of Enquiry report and the relevant files to apportion blame and recommend surcharge, write-off or preventive measures.
